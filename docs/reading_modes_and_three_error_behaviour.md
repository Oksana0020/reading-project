# Reader Leader Reading Modes and Three-Error Behaviour

> **Short answer:** A child is **not permanently blocked** after three incorrect attempts. The experience changes by mode: Assisted keeps supportive correction available, Guided provides a model after two unsuccessful attempts, and Monthly Assessment records the first pass quietly while reading continues.

## Reader Leader: the three in-session modes

The three chips in the reading canvas—**Assisted**, **Guided**, and **Monthly**—are in-session feedback modes. They control what the child sees while reading the same story; they are not reading levels or separate content libraries.

| Mode | What the child sees | What happens after three incorrect attempts at a word | Can the child continue? | What is saved for adults? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Assisted Practice** | The active word is highlighted. An incorrect attempt is shown as a gentle red correction state. A correct reread changes to blue with a green self-correction marker. | There is **no automatic three-strike lockout**. The word remains a practice point until it is read correctly or the child uses the explicit practice support to move on. | **Yes.** The child can self-correct, use the available practice controls, or complete guided practice rather than being trapped on one word. | Attempts, retries, self-corrections, and practice words. The report rewards the correction, not merely the first error. |
| **Guided Practice** | It uses the Assisted visual support plus a Reading Coach. | After **two** unsuccessful attempts, the coach pauses live listening and offers a model-audio prompt. The third attempt happens after that support. If it is still not correct, the word remains a kind practice point; there is no automatic failure or permanent stop. | **Yes.** The child can try again, use the model, use the explicit practice support, or finish through the guided-practice completion path. | Retries and the model-prompt event, so a teacher can see which words may need another small practice session. |
| **Monthly Assessment** | No red correction, retry prompt, or model interruption is shown. The child reads continuously. | The first-pass mismatch is recorded silently for later teacher review. A repeated incorrect word does **not** create an on-screen correction sequence. | **Yes—automatically.** Each recognised reading step advances the quiet first-pass record so the child can continue the story without interruption. | First-pass Story Match %, first-pass WCPM, and quiet review events. Practice/retry prompts are deliberately excluded. |

In Assisted and Guided Practice, an incorrect attempt does not automatically advance the expected word, because the purpose is to allow a calm correction. In Monthly Assessment, the expected word advances even after a mismatch, because the purpose is an uninterrupted first-read sample. All three modes retain a clear finish path; Reader Leader should never create a punitive “stuck” state for a child.

A transcript mismatch is a **practice or teacher-review signal**, not proof that a child mispronounced a word. ## Practical choice for an adult

Choose **Assisted Practice** for ordinary coached reading where the child benefits from visible correction and chances to retry. Choose **Guided Practice** when the child may need a model after a couple of tries. Choose **Monthly Assessment** only when the goal is a quiet, uninterrupted first-pass sample for a teacher to discuss later, rather than real-time coaching.

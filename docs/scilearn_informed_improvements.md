# Reader Leader: Platform-Informed Improvement Opportunities

**Prepared by:** Manus AI  
**Date:** 7 September 2026  
**Reference scope:** The recommendations draw on a reference-only review of the user-authorised [SciLearn learner experience](https://ondemand2.scilearn.com/slc/learner?tenantId=408599&schoolOrgId=312609). No third-party learner records were copied, edited, or retained.

## Summary

Reader Leader already provides a strong foundation for guided read-aloud practice, child/teacher/parent role separation, teacher-created materials, session review, Irish English safeguards, and family encouragement. The reviewed platform reinforces the value of separating assigned work, optional practice, and reference resources. The recommended additions below would make Reader Leader easier for children to understand, more useful for teachers to manage, and more transparent for families, while preserving its supportive and non-diagnostic approach.

| Priority | Recommendation | Why it fits Reader Leader | Suggested safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Add a **Practice Lab** for short optional fluency activities. | It gives children a clear place to rehearse a skill without confusing practice with teacher-assigned reading. | Label every activity “Try it out — not part of your reading report” and exclude it from WCPM and monthly-assessment calculations. |
| High | Add a **Reading Toolkit** with printable child, teacher, and parent supports. | Micro-guides for before/during/after reading, microphone setup, and word-play routines would reduce avoidable friction. | Keep generic resources separate from individual learner records and use age-appropriate language. |
| High | Add a **session-start check** that combines microphone, recognition, and connection readiness. | The new connection indicator can become an actionable pre-flight check that explains what is ready and what guided-practice alternative remains available. | Do not imply that connection quality is a measure of reading ability or ASR accuracy. |
| Medium | Create teacher-curated **level-and-skill practice sets**. | Teachers could offer short drills for phrasing, punctuation pauses, high-frequency words, and vocabulary rehearsal alongside longer stories. | Teachers choose or assign materials; no automatic ability grouping based on speech results. |
| Medium | Add a small **weekly goal and completion trail**. | The child dashboard already has a warm progress view that could support realistic practice habits. | Allow family/teacher adjustment and avoid punitive streak loss, leaderboards, or public comparison. |
| Medium | Offer **activity-specific help cards** and short walkthroughs. | A “How this reading mode works” card near each activity can help children use guided, assisted, and monthly modes confidently. | State clearly that live browser recognition may be unavailable or delayed and that teacher review remains important. |
| Later | Add a **teacher review queue with prioritisation**. | The existing Pending Speech Matches tab could be extended with filters for recency, learner, session type, and unreviewed audio availability. | Present flags as prompts for professional review, not automatic errors or diagnoses. |
| Later | Add a **family reading conversation pack**. | Parents could download or view short discussion prompts matched to a story and celebrate effort rather than raw scores. | Do not expose teacher-only provisional-match or internal assessment detail. |

## Suggested Child Information Architecture

The child experience would benefit from three plainly named destinations: **My stories**, **Practice Lab**, and **Reading Toolkit**. My stories would retain teacher-assigned passages and saved progress. Practice Lab would contain optional, short, clearly non-assessed fluency tasks. Reading Toolkit would provide friendly strategy cards, a microphone/connection check, and printable help for home reading.

> Optional practice should be visibly marked as exploratory and excluded from formal reading reports. This preserves the difference between learning through rehearsal and teacher-reviewed assessment.

## Suggested Delivery Sequence

The first delivery should build the Practice Lab shell, resource cards, and session-start readiness check because these features directly respond to the observed latency concern. The second delivery should add teacher-curated practice sets and guided help cards. The third delivery can extend progress trails and family conversation supports after teacher and parent feedback confirms that the new information architecture feels manageable.

## Design Principles to Preserve

The product should remain encouraging, calm, and transparent. It should avoid ranking children, diagnosing pronunciation or accent, and treating browser speech recognition as a high-stakes assessment source. Teacher judgement, child dignity, and clear family explanations remain the strongest design constraints for every new feature.
